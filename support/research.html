Research and evidence register

This register links each external source to the decision it supports and states what it cannot establish. Original analysis consists of the cohort design, category and lever hypotheses, order-cost ledger, demand/displacement model and controlled pilot proposal.

Evidence status: secondary research, supplied case analysis and functional prototype verification. No factory interviews, recruited suppliers, survey responses, live traffic tests, achieved savings or retention outcomes are claimed.

Research protocol and proposed primary work

Review government factory frames and cluster documentation. Compare official platform mechanisms. Inspect manufacturer process disclosures. Audit the supplied prototype’s actual user paths and rule definitions. Triangulate these sources with the supplied case’s acquisition and first-30-day retention problem.

Before launch, conduct ten factory calls across four offline, three online-elsewhere and three previously churned manufacturers, plus five matched buyer-offer checks. This work is proposed and has not been performed.

  1. Inspect the latest B2B invoice and online settlement, where available. Reconstruct contribution on an identical pack.
  2. Ask which stages the factory owns, which are job-worked, and how repeat costs change with a 60-pack consumer batch.
  3. Observe unit packing and label printing. Measure minutes, defects and operator availability.
  4. Reconstruct a failed order: outbound fee, returned condition, reverse cost, usable stock and time to cash.
  5. Record the minimum profitable B2C commitment, reasons for refusal and willingness to commit a second batch without subsidy.
  6. Match checkout offers on pack count, dimensions, fabric/GSM, finishing, destination, service and all buyer charges.

Interpretation safeguards

  • The national 23,838 formal operating-factory universe includes upstream textile activity and excludes many informal units. It is not an eligible manufacturer TAM.
  • The Panipat 288 proxy uses a factory count and category share from a sample plan. Reconcile source-register definitions before treating it as a supplier list.
  • Scores rank hypotheses, not observed success probabilities. Hard cost and service gates override high scores.
  • The model’s ₹250 benchmark is illustrative. It is not the average of unmatched live listings.
  • Return share in economics uses dispatched orders. The prototype return alert uses delivered orders. With 10 returns and 15 RTO per 100 dispatched, the corresponding delivered denominator is 85, so the delivered return share is 11.76%.
  • Attribution is not incrementality. Net kept value subtracts displaced sales at the old ₹250 price, then official NMV requires tax and return-timing reconciliation.
  • Operating productivity compares assumed workloads. It is a testable goal, not measured Meesho staffing savings.

Why these category and lever scores

Cover-only home textiles offer a plausible combination of controllable fabric/process costs, compact shipping and specification standardisation. Apparel adds size/fit and variant complexity. Moulded storage can lose advantage to volumetric freight. Footwear adds fit and stock-size risk. Filled bedding increases delivery burden. Beauty requires category-specific authenticity and product controls. These are decision hypotheses requiring cost and quality evidence, not measured category return rates.

The initial lever ranking weights impact 35%, confidence 25%, speed 20% and scalability 20%. Batch ledger/refill gate scores 4.55; assisted catalogue/dispatch setup 4.00; pooled dispatch 2.90; blanket price subsidy 2.75; inventory buyback 2.05. Confidence reflects the specificity of the mechanism and feasibility of testing, not a statistical probability. Re-score after the first cohort and record sensitivity to weights.
